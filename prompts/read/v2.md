Write a Card for the paper above: a structured summary the physicist will review in about 4 minutes to decide whether the paper matters to them. The JSON schema gives the shape; these rules say how to fill it.

## Quotes

Every factual field carries `evidence`: one or two quotes from the paper text that back its value. A program checks every quote against the paper text, and a quote that is not found makes the field unsupported.

- Copy the quote character for character from the paper text, LaTeX included: keep `$...$`, `\cite{...}`, `~`, `\%` and macros exactly as written. Do not fix typos, expand macros, or turn LaTeX into Unicode.
- One quote is one contiguous span of 5 to 60 words inside a single paragraph. Never join pieces, never use `...` or `[...]`, never cross a section heading or a blank line.
- Prefer the sentence that states the fact most directly: a sentence in the results or a caption over the abstract or introduction.
- Do not write where the quote is from. The program finds that.

Write the `evidence` first, then the `value` based on it. Set `inferred` to true when the value is not stated in your quotes but follows from them: you combined two quotes, converted a number, or concluded something the paper doesn't say in so many words. A value that only rewords a quote is not inferred. Every number or parameter you write in a value must appear in that field's own quotes; if it comes from elsewhere in the paper, add a quote for it or set `inferred` to true.

## Wording

These rules hold for every text field of the Card, `opinion` included.

- Expand every acronym the first time the Card uses it, in field order, and use the acronym after that: "Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)". Do this even where the paper does not.
- Name the authors by surname: "Surname et al." with the first author's surname, both surnames when there are two ("Albergo and Kanwar"), or the surname alone for a single author. Never write "the authors". The authors of this paper, from the arXiv metadata, are: {{authors}}

Quotes are the exception: they are copied exactly, acronyms and all.

## Fields

- `tldr`: two sentences. What they did, and the main result with its number if there is one.
- `key_results`: 1 to 5 results, most important first. Keep numbers, units and error bars exactly as the paper states them, including the system size or parameters they hold for. Do not round, convert or combine numbers. Every number you write in a result value must appear in the paper text.
- `assumptions`: up to 4 assumptions the results rest on (approximations, regimes, choices the conclusions depend on), stated or clearly used by the paper.
- `system`: the physical system and the parameters studied (model, dimension, lattice sizes, couplings).
- `method`: the method the paper proposes or uses, in one or two sentences.
- `baselines`: what the method is compared against, or null if it is compared against nothing.
- `claimed_advance`: what the authors say is new or better than prior work, in their terms.
- `limitations_stated`: up to 4 limitations the authors themselves state.
- `key_equations`: up to 3 equations a reader needs to follow the paper. Give the `\label` key, the equation body copied from the source, and one line on what it means.
- `key_figures`: up to 3 figures worth seeing, chosen from their captions. Give the figure environment's `\label` key and one line on why it is worth seeing. If the paper text has no labelled figures, leave this empty.
- `code_data`: a link to code or data if the paper gives one, or null.
- `questions`: which of the research questions below this paper bears on, each with one line on why. Leave it empty if none applies.

## Opinion

`opinion` is the only place for your own judgement, and your judgement goes nowhere else. Every other field reports what the paper says.

- `limitations_flagged`: up to 3 weaknesses you see that the authors do not state: missing baselines, small systems, results that may not scale, untested assumptions. Do not repeat `limitations_stated`.
- `deep_read`: whether this paper deserves a full read by someone working on the research questions below, and one line on why. Recommend it rarely: only for papers that change how someone working on these questions would think or work.
